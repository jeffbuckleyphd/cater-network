The CATER model

The Concept

A distributed centre designed to support, connect, and strengthen existing technology education research communities.

Facilitative, not centralising

CATER is proposed as a distributed centre for technology education research. It is not intended to replace existing research groups, define the boundaries of the field, or act as a gatekeeper. Its purpose is to provide shared infrastructure through which researchers can connect, develop methods, exchange tools, build capacity, and collaborate more easily across institutional and national contexts.

Distributed by Design

CATER is built around existing researchers, groups, institutions, and contexts. It is intended to connect and support activity across the field, rather than concentrate it in one location.

Infrastructure for Others

The centre focuses on tools, methods, training, protocols, resources, and collaborative structures that others can adapt, use, critique, and develop further.

Collective Capacity

CATER aims to make larger forms of collaboration more feasible, including working groups, research clinics, multi-site studies, grant development, and shared dissemination.

From Hidden Work to Shared Infrastructure

A substantial amount of scholarly work takes place before research findings are produced. Researchers define constructs, design instruments, develop protocols, make methodological decisions, build tools, and create analytical frameworks. Yet much of this work remains hidden inside individual projects, appendices, supplementary files, or local research groups.

CATER is intended to help make this work more visible, reusable, and open to further development. This does not mean standardising the field around a single approach. Rather, it means creating better conditions for researchers to share the infrastructure they develop, learn from each other’s methodological decisions, and build stronger cumulative foundations for technology education research.

Local Scholarly Work

Constructs, instruments, protocols, tools, datasets, coding schemes, analytical frameworks, and methodological decisions developed within individual projects.

CATER Infrastructure Layer

A shared space for methods articles, open tools, training resources, working groups, textbooks, research clinics, and collaborative methodological development.

Field-Level Reuse

Stronger studies, clearer constructs, reusable resources, shared protocols, multi-site collaborations, and more credible technology education research.

Why this matters

When methodological and conceptual work remains hidden, the field risks repeated reinvention, fragmented construct definitions, and inconsistent operationalisations of important ideas. CATER would provide a structure through which this work can be shared without requiring researchers to give up their own agendas, contexts, or theoretical commitments.

Developing the Network

A Shared Structure for Stronger Research

CATER will develop through collaboration with researchers, educators, and partners who want to build shared infrastructure, support methodological capability, and create opportunities for larger collective work in technology education research.
